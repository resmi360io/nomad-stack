## B. Claims checked against primary sources

### B1. Row 145: "Google no longer supports FAQ schema"

**Confirmed. The date is May 2026, not this week.**

What Google's documentation changelog (https://developers.google.com/search/updates) says:
- **2026-05-08, "Deprecating the FAQ rich result feature":** "This feature will no longer appear in Google Search starting May 7, 2026."
- **2026-06-15, "Removing documentation for the FAQ rich result feature":** "The FAQ rich result feature is no longer shown in Google Search results."
- The old FAQPage doc URL now lands on that changelog.
- The structured-data gallery (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/search-gallery) no longer lists FAQ.
- I found no Search Central blog post about it, which is why the blog-only watch missed it.

What I could not confirm from Google directly:
- The Search Console report and Rich Results Test being dropped in June 2026, and API support ending in August 2026. These are quoted from Google's now-removed doc by PPC Land (2026-05-10).
- Whether Google says to keep or remove the markup. Neither changelog entry says.

Primary guidance that still applies:
- The structured data policies (updated 2026-07-10) say "Don't mark up content that is not visible to readers of the page."
- When Google retired other types on 2025-06-12, it said "This update won't affect how pages are ranked" (https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/06/simplifying-search-results).
- Bing's position on FAQPage: not checked; I found no primary statement this run.

**What this means for the site:**
- **No site change.** Keep the markup. Removing it gains nothing, costs a release, and REVIEW mode treats JSON-LD removal as a protected regression.
- **One correction to your brief:** `scripts/seo/checks.mjs` does not require FAQ schema. It errors only when FAQ schema is present and its text is not visible on the page (lines 74 to 80). That check still enforces Google's general visible-content rule, so it stays.
- **Knowledge-file fixes:** correct tactics 21 and 28, and add the documentation changelog to the watchlist and to the skill's update sources.
- The weekly FAQ work stays. It is for readers and AI answers, never for rich results.

### B2. Row 133: Google timing figures

**No Google page found.** The figures were shown by Gary Illyes at Search Central Live Deep Dive Europe in Barcelona on 2026-10-02. They are known only through a recap by a community speaker (John Campbell, ROAST), reported by:
- Search Engine Journal, 2026-10-04: https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-crawling-indexing-recovery-timing-ranges/591829/
- PPC Land, 2026-10-03: https://ppc.land/google-says-new-pages-take-about-20-hours-to-be-found-some-never-are/

Neither gives a method, sample, or definition of "typical". The video's numbers match the recap:

| Event | Typical | Slowest |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery of a new URL | about 20 hours | weeks to never |
| Refresh of a known URL | about 30 days | weeks to never |
| Indexing | about 1.5 hours | months or never |
| Title change | 1 to 2 days | weeks to months |
| Search Console removal | about 2 hours | 24 hours |
| Canonical change | 1 to 3 weeks | months |
| Core-update recovery | 3 to 6 months | 6 months to a year |

The primary source for recovery is Google's core-updates page (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates, updated 2025-12-10):
- "some changes can take effect in a few days, but it could take several months for our systems to learn and confirm"
- wait "at least a full week after a core update completes before analyzing your site in Search Console"

Verdict: record them in the measurement rules as reported figures, labelled as not primary. They do not shorten rule 3. The 30-day refresh figure is the useful one: it is why requesting indexing matters before a measurement clock can start.
